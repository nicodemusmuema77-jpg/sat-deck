Digital SAT pacing guide

An even pace works out to about 71 seconds per Reading and Writing question and 95 seconds per Math question. That average is a checkpoint, not a rule for every question: some questions take less time and leave room for harder ones.

How to practice with the timer

  1. Choose the section that matches your practice module and start both together.
  2. Update “Question you are on” at a checkpoint instead of after every question.
  3. If you are behind, flag the current problem, make your best choice, and protect time for the remaining questions.
  4. After the module, review why you lost time. Use the strategy deck or active-recall quiz to practice that skill.

Frequently asked questions

How much time do you get per question on the Digital SAT?

About 71 seconds per question in Reading and Writing (32 minutes for 27 questions) and about 95 seconds per question in Math (35 minutes for 22 questions). These are section-wide averages, so individual questions can take more or less time.

How is the Digital SAT timed?

Each section is split into two separately timed modules. Reading and Writing is two 32-minute modules of 27 questions; Math is two 35-minute modules of 22 questions. Once a module's time is up you cannot return to it.

Can I move back and forth between questions?

Yes. Within a module you can flag questions and return to them before that module's time runs out. This timer covers one module at a time.

Should I use this timer on the real SAT?

No. Use it only for independent practice. During the real SAT, Bluebook displays the official countdown and moves you between separately timed modules.

Module lengths follow the standard Digital SAT: Reading & Writing is two 32-minute modules of 27 questions; Math is two 35-minute modules of 22 questions. This timer covers one module at a time. It does not submit anything or sync with Bluebook — it is just a stopwatch with a pace target.
